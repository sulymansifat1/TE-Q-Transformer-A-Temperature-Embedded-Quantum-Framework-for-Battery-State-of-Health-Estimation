ARTICLE                  INFO                              ABSTRACT

Keywords:                                                  The tremendous success of deep learning and transfer learning broadened the scope of fault diagnosis,
Fault diagnosis                                            especially the latter further improved the diagnosis accuracy under multiple working conditions. However,
Bearings                                                   most existing attempts assume that label distribution is domain-invariant despite taking into account the
Imbalanced domain adaptation
                                                           different feature distributions. This does not accommodate the diversity of fault mode distributions under
Label shift
                                                           different operating conditions and weakens the generalization to imbalanced domain adaptation (IDA) scenar-
Domain shift
                                                           ios. Therefore, this work proposed a novel deep imbalanced domain adaptation (DIDA) framework for fault
                                                           diagnosis of bearings, aiming at the challenging scenario where feature shift and label shift exist simultaneously
                                                           under different working conditions. Specifically, DIDA overcomes the class-imbalanced label shift and achieves
                                                           a fine-grained latent space matching by cost-sensitive learning and categorical alignment. Besides, margin
                                                           loss regularization is introduced to further optimize classification boundaries and improve cross-domain
                                                           generalization for IDA fault diagnosis tasks. Finally, we simulated the IDA protocols on experimental datasets
                                                           and conducted case studies under multiple working conditions, thus validating the effectiveness and superiority
                                                           of the proposed framework.

                                                                                           superiority with experimental datasets. The main contributions of this

                                                                                                 All scenarios were repeatedly trained and tested 10 times. Then, the
                                                                                            results of DIDA on the CWRU dataset are shown in Table 7. The results
                                                                                            show that the proposed DIDA still works well on the CWRU dataset
                                                                                            with 10 categories. In addition, similar to the previous case study, DIDA
                                                                                            performance varies on different IDA tasks. Specifically, the tasks with
                                                                                            imbalanced datasets as the source domain of the training set, i.e., I2B
                                                                                            and I2I, are more challenging. Differently, this performance difference
                                                                                            is greater on the CWRU dataset than that on the self-built dataset,
                                                                                            perhaps because more categories increase the difficulty of imbalanced
                                                                                            classification. The distribution of extracted features also shows this
                                                                                            difference, as plotted in Fig. 11. More categories in the CWRU dataset,
                                                                                            i.e., multiple failure modes with varying degrees, present a significant
                                                                                            challenge for classification with the I2I protocol. The features extracted
                                                                                            by the baseline are more difficult to distinguish between classes with
                                                                                            the same fault mode but different degrees. In addition, it seems that
                                                                                            label shift in the CWRU datasets creates more trouble than feature
                                                                                            shift for learning discriminative features, thus even different classes of
                                                                                            samples in the source domain appear to be confounded. DAN obtains
                                                                                            better results than baseline, but extraction of domain-invariant features
                                                                                            on categories with large differences in number is still not satisfactory.
                                                                                            However, the proposed DIDA overcomes the challenge of larger label
                                                                                            shift on CWRU datasets, and extracts more generalizable features with
                                                                                            the I2I protocol.
