1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & MOTIVATION
========================================================================================
Lithium-ion battery State of Health (SOH = C_k / C_0) is the cornerstone metric for 
safe, reliable, and durable operation in electric vehicles (EVs) and energy storage 
systems (BESS). Battery degradation is governed by complex electrochemical phenomena:
  - At elevated temperatures (>35°C): Accelerated Solid Electrolyte Interphase (SEI) 
    growth, binder decomposition, and transition metal dissolution.
  - At sub-ambient temperatures (<15°C): Sluggish ionic diffusivity in the electrolyte, 
    elevated charge-transfer resistance, and severe metallic Lithium Plating.

CRITICAL PROBLEM IN EXISTING LITERATURE:
Almost all data-driven deep learning models (LSTM, GRU, CNN, vanilla Transformer, 
and even standard Quantum Neural Networks) treat Temperature (T) as an ordinary 
numerical channel, blindly applying Min-Max normalization alongside Voltage and Current. 
Consequently, they fail to model the non-linear Arrhenius thermodynamics governing 
battery degradation, leading to poor generalization across different thermal regimes 
and unseen battery cells.

PROPOSED SOLUTION (TE-Q-TRANSFORMER):
A physics-guided hybrid quantum-classical architecture that directly integrates 
Arrhenius degradation thermodynamics into the rotation parameters of a 4-qubit quantum 
variational circuit with a rich multi-body entangler topology, coupled with a 
local-temporal smoothing operator (Conv1D) and global sequence attention (Transformer Encoder).

2. CORE RESEARCH CONTRIBUTIONS (AMADER CONTRIBUTION KISE?)
========================================================================================
Our contributions in proposing TE-Q-Transformer are categorized into five core pillars:

1. NOVEL PHYSICS-INFORMED ARRHENIUS QUANTUM TEMPERATURE GATE:
   Instead of treating temperature as an arbitrary normalized feature, we design an 
   Arrhenius-guided gate inside the quantum embedding layer. Raw Celsius temperature 
   is mapped to Kelvin and drives two competing degradation kinetics:
     a) SEI Growth Factor (high-temperature acceleration).
     b) Lithium Plating Factor (low-temperature acceleration).
   These degradation mechanics are modulated by two trainable activation-energy parameters 
   (Ea_sei and Ea_pl) learned via end-to-end backpropagation, producing a physics-informed 
   rotation angle theta_T for the quantum state.

2. HIGH-EXPRESSIVITY "RICH" QUANTUM ENTANGLER TOPOLOGY:
   Standard Quantum Machine Learning (QML) models rely on trivial basic entanglement 
   (e.g., circular CNOT rings). We engineer a "Rich Entangler" incorporating:
     - Parametric Rz rotations (phase control),
     - Adjacent CNOT gates (entanglement),
     - Parametric Ising ZZ (Rzz) coupling gates (direct feature-to-feature non-linear interaction),
     - 3-qubit Toffoli gates (multi-body cross-sensor non-linear correlation),
     - Hadamard superposition layers.
   This enables the 4-qubit quantum circuit to map multi-sensor interactions (Voltage, 
   Current, Time, and Arrhenius Temperature) into a high-dimensional non-linear Hilbert space.

3. TWO-TIER TEMPORAL HIERARCHY (CONV1D LOCAL FILTER + TRANSFORMER GLOBAL ATTENTION):
   Raw battery sensor sequences contain high-frequency noise from charge-discharge dynamics. 
   We introduce a hierarchical processing pipeline:
     - Tier 1 (Local): A 1D Convolutional filter (kernel size = 3) smooths high-frequency 
       short-term quantum feature fluctuations along the sequence length.
     - Tier 2 (Global): A Multi-Head Self-Attention Transformer encoder with a learnable 
       [CLS] token captures long-range inter-cycle ageing trajectories across all 512 steps.

4. FIRST RIGOROUS CLASSICAL VS. QUANTUM INDUCTIVE BIAS VALIDATION:
   We demonstrate that the quantum representation is not just a cosmetic addition. In a 
   strictly capacity-matched comparison (identical parameter budget ~92.5k, identical 
   Arrhenius physics, identical downstream Transformer):
     - Quantum + Physics achieves Macro RMSE 0.0215, R^2 = 0.610.
     - Classical MLP + Physics collapses to Macro RMSE 0.0431, R^2 = -0.746.
   This provides empirical evidence of quantum inductive bias for non-linear degradation.

5. STRICT CROSS-CELL & TEMPORAL BENCHMARKING WITH HONEST ZERO-SHOT DISCLOSURE:
   We evaluate on the NASA benchmark with strict cross-cell separation:
     - B0018 (24°C nominal) -> Unseen Cell Test
     - B0032 (43°C elevated) -> Unseen Cell Test
     - B0053 (4°C sub-ambient) -> Temporal extrapolation tail (last 30% cycles)
   Furthermore, we honestly test zero-shot transferability to CALCE CS2 cells without 
   cherry-picking, transparently establishing the domain boundaries of cross-chemistry transfer.

3. WHY DOES TE-Q-TRANSFORMER PERFORM SO WELL? (KEY FEATURE BREAKDOWN)
========================================================================================
Why does TE-Q-Transformer outperform LSTM, GRU, CNN1D, TCN, vanilla Transformer, 
PatchTST, iTransformer, QLSTM, and QNN-GRU? 

The performance superiority stems from the synergistic interaction of 5 specific features:

FEATURE 1: Non-Linear Thermodynamic Inductive Bias (Arrhenius Encoding)
  - Ordinary models assume a monotonic linear or generic non-linear relationship for temperature.
  - In reality, 4°C causes Lithium plating, 24°C is benign, and 43°C causes SEI growth.
  - TE-Q-Transformer's dual-activation Arrhenius formulation (sei_term + plating_term) 
    gives the neural network an exact physical blueprint of U-shaped degradation curves.
  - Ablation proof: Physics-guided encoding improves macro RMSE by 16.5% over conventional 
    temperature normalization (0.0168 vs. 0.0201).

FEATURE 2: Rich Entangler Capturing Multi-Sensor Cross-Terms in Hilbert Space
  - In battery aging, degradation is never caused by voltage or temperature in isolation; 
    it is the joint product (e.g., high charging current under low temperature causes plating; 
    high voltage at elevated temperature causes cathode cracking).
  - Classical linear maps require huge MLP widths to model these high-order cross-products.
  - Our 4-qubit Rich Entangler uses Ising ZZ coupling and Toffoli gates to natively represent 
    multi-variable correlations in quantum state space.
  - Ablation proof: Replacing Rich Entangler with Basic Entangler causes the single largest 
    error explosion in the entire architecture (+0.0221 RMSE increase, jumping from 0.0168 to 0.0388).

FEATURE 3: Local Temporal Smoothing (Conv1D) Eliminating Sensor Jitter
  - Transformers lack local inductive bias (they treat sequence tokens as an unordered set 
    without local continuity unless guided).
  - Individual quantum expectation readouts <Z_i> can exhibit micro-variations.
  - The Conv1d (k=3, pad=1) acts as a physical moving-average filter across consecutive discharge points.
  - Ablation proof: Removing the Conv1d smoother worsens RMSE from 0.0168 to 0.0261 (+0.0093 increase).

FEATURE 4: Global Sequence Representation via Learnable [CLS] Token
  - Classical recurrent models (LSTM/GRU) rely on the last hidden state h_L. If the final 
    discharge points are noisy or cut off at the cut-off voltage (e.g., 2.7V or 2.5V), 
    the prediction degrades severely.
  - TE-Q-Transformer prepends a learnable [CLS] token that attends globally across all 512 
    discharge time steps via Multi-Head Self-Attention.
  - Ablation proof: Mean pooling instead of CLS token worsens RMSE from 0.0168 to 0.0237 (+0.0069 increase).

FEATURE 5: Sinusoidal Positional Encoding Preserving Discharge Kinetics
  - Discharge voltage curves follow a distinct three-phase trajectory (initial ohmic drop, 
    plateau phase, and steep concentration polarization drop).
  - Without positional encoding, self-attention cannot differentiate between early discharge 
    and end-of-discharge states.
  - Ablation proof: Removing Positional Encoding degrades RMSE from 0.0168 to 0.0330 (+0.0162 increase).

4. FULL MATHEMATICAL & ARCHITECTURAL FORMULATION (5-PHASE PIPELINE)
========================================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 1: INPUT SENSOR SEQUENCE PREPROCESSING
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input window: X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times L \times 4}
  - B: Batch size
  - L = 512: Intra-cycle discharge sequence length
  - 4 Channels:
      Channel 0: Voltage (V)           -> Min-Max scaled to [0, 1] using training set only.
      Channel 1: Current (I)           -> Min-Max scaled to [0, 1] using training set only.
      Channel 2: Temperature (T_c)     -> RAW Celsius (°C), strictly unscaled!
      Channel 3: Time (t_norm)         -> Normalized intra-cycle time [0, 1].

Flattening for time-step parallel quantum processing:
  X_flat = reshape(X, [B * L, 4])

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 2: PHYSICS-INFORMED QUANTUM EMBEDDING (ARRHENIUS GATE + 4-QUBIT VQC)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 2A: Thermodynamic Conversion to Kelvin
  T_K = clamp(T_c + 273.15, min = 1.0)        [Prevents non-physical negative Kelvin]

Step 2B: Arrhenius Degradation Factors
  Constants:
    - Reference Temperature: T_ref = 298.15 K (25°C)
    - Universal Gas Constant: R = 8.314462618 J / (mol * K)
  Trainable Parameters:
    - Ea_sei: Activation energy scaling factor for SEI growth (initialized at 3.0)
    - Ea_pl:  Activation energy scaling factor for Lithium Plating (initialized at 3.0)
  Actual Activation Energies (J/mol):
    - E_sei = Ea_sei * 10,000.0
    - E_pl  = Ea_pl  * 10,000.0

  1. High-Temperature SEI Growth Acceleration Factor:
       phi_sei = exp[ (E_sei / R) * ( (1 / T_ref) - (1 / T_K) ) ]
       * When T_K > T_ref, (1/T_ref - 1/T_K) > 0, so phi_sei exponentially increases.

  2. Low-Temperature Lithium Plating Factor:
       phi_pl  = exp[ (E_pl / R)  * ( (1 / T_K) - (1 / T_ref) ) ]
       * When T_K < T_ref, (1/T_K - 1/T_ref) > 0, so phi_pl exponentially increases.

  3. Combined Thermal Degradation Factor:
       phi = phi_sei + phi_pl

Step 2C: Quantum Angle Construction
  - Voltage Angle:     theta_V = V * pi              \in [0, pi]
  - Current Angle:     theta_I = I * pi              \in [0, pi]
  - Time Angle:        theta_t = t_norm * pi         \in [0, pi]
  - Temperature Angle: theta_T = (pi / 4.0) * phi    [Physics-modulated rotation]

  Angle Vector: Theta = [theta_V, theta_I, theta_t, theta_T] \in \mathbb{R}^{(B*L) \times 4}

Step 2D: 4-Qubit Variational Quantum Circuit (PennyLane default.qubit)
  Initial State: |0000> = |0>^{\otimes 4}

  1. State Encoding Unitary U_enc:
     Apply Pauli-Y rotations to each wire:
       q0: Ry(theta_V)
       q1: Ry(theta_I)
       q2: Ry(theta_t)
       q3: Ry(theta_T)

  2. Rich Entangler Unitary U_rich:
     Trainable parameters:
       - W_ent \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 4} (qubit Rz rotation angles)
       - W_rzz \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 3} (Ising ZZ coupling angles)

     Circuit Topology:
       a) Phase Modulation:
          Apply Rz(W_ent[q]) on each wire q \in {0, 1, 2, 3}.
       b) Nearest-Neighbor Entanglement:
          Apply CNOT(wires=[q, q+1]) for q \in {0, 1, 2}.
       c) Parametric Ising Interaction (Two-Body Correlations):
          Apply IsingZZ(W_rzz[q], wires=[q, q+1]) for q \in {0, 1, 2}.
       d) Three-Body Non-Linear Mixing (Toffoli / CCNOT):
          Apply Toffoli(wires=[0, 1, 2])
          Apply Toffoli(wires=[1, 2, 3])
       e) Superposition & Final Phase Shift:
          Apply Hadamard(q) on each wire q \in {0, 1, 2, 3}.
          Apply Rz(W_ent[q]) on each wire q \in {0, 1, 2, 3}.

  3. Quantum Expectation Readout:
     Measure expectation value of Pauli-Z operator on all 4 wires:
       q_out = [ <psi| Z_0 |psi>, <psi| Z_1 |psi>, <psi| Z_2 |psi>, <psi| Z_3 |psi> ]^T
       Output: q_tensor \in [-1, 1]^{(B*L) \times 4}

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 3: TEMPORAL FEATURE REFINEMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 3A: Linear Projection into Latent Transformer Space
  W_p \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 4},  b_p \in \mathbb{R}^{64}
  h_flat = q_tensor * W_p^T + b_p              -> [(B*L), 64]
  h_seq  = reshape(h_flat, [B, L, 64])         -> [B, 512, 64]

Step 3B: Local Temporal Convolution (Noise Smoothing)
  Transpose for Conv1d: h_transposed = transpose(h_seq, 1, 2)  -> [B, 64, 512]
  Apply 1D Convolution:
    - in_channels = 64, out_channels = 64
    - kernel_size = 3, padding = 1, bias = False
  h_smooth = Conv1d(h_transposed)                              -> [B, 64, 512]
  Restore shape: h_refined = transpose(h_smooth, 1, 2)         -> [B, 512, 64]

Step 3C: CLS Token Prepending & Positional Encoding
  1. Learnable CLS token: z_cls_param \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 1 \times 64}
     Expand across batch: z_cls_batch \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1 \times 64}
     Concatenate:
       Z_seq = concat([z_cls_batch, h_refined], dim=1)         -> [B, 513, 64]

  2. Sinusoidal Positional Encoding:
       PE(pos, 2i)   = sin( pos / 10000^{2i / 64} )
       PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos( pos / 10000^{2i / 64} )
       Z_pos = Z_seq + PE[: 513, :]                            -> [B, 513, 64]

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 4: TRANSFORMER ENCODER (GLOBAL ATTENTION)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Architecture: nn.TransformerEncoder with 3 stacked layers.
  - d_model = 64
  - n_heads = 2  (per-head dimension d_k = 32)
  - dim_feedforward = 64
  - Activation: GELU
  - Normalization: Pre-LayerNorm (norm_first = True)
  - Dropout = 0.0

Multi-Head Attention Equation:
  Q = Z_pos W_Q,  K = Z_pos W_K,  V = Z_pos W_V
  Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( (Q K^T) / sqrt(d_k) ) V

After 3 layers of Pre-LN Multi-Head Self-Attention and Feed-Forward Networks:
  Z_encoded = TransformerEncoder(Z_pos)                       -> [B, 513, 64]

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 5: SOH OUTPUT HEAD & REGRESSION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 5A: CLS Pooling
  Extract the 0-th token representing the entire 512-step sequence:
  z_cls_out = Z_encoded[:, 0, :]                              -> [B, 64]

Step 5B: MLP Regression Head
  Layer 1: Linear(64 -> 64)
  Activation: GELU()
  Dropout: Dropout(p = 0.0)
  Layer 2: Linear(64 -> 1)

  y_hat = Linear_2( GELU( Linear_1( z_cls_out ) ) )           -> [B, 1]
  Predicted SOH = squeeze(y_hat, -1)                          -> [B] (Scalar SOH per cycle)

8. BOUNDARY CONDITIONS, CROSS-CELL GENERALIZATION & CALCE ZERO-SHOT FINDINGS

Scientific rigor requires identifying not only where a model succeeds, but also where 
its domain boundaries lie:

1. NASA MULTI-SEED RUN-TO-RUN VARIABILITY:
   - Across 5 seeds (42 to 46), TE-Q-Transformer achieves a mean RMSE of 0.0244 ± 0.0096.
   - Best run: Seed 44 (RMSE 0.0150).
   - Worst run: Seed 43 (RMSE 0.0405).
   - This indicates that while TE-Q-Transformer has the lowest expected error, variational 
     quantum circuits can experience optimization landscape sensitivity (e.g., barren plateaus 
     or local minima in parameter space).

2. ZERO-SHOT CALCE TRANSFER (EXTERNAL VALIDATION):
   - When tested zero-shot on 4 CALCE CS2 cells (3,909 cycles total) without fine-tuning:
       Pooled RMSE = 0.3325, MAE = 0.2676, R^2 = -1.789.
   - Root Causes of Transfer Limitation:
     a) Chemistry Mismatch: NASA uses cylindrical 18650 cells (LiCoO2 / graphite, 2.1 Ah), 
        while CALCE CS2 uses prismatic pouch cells (LiCoO2, 1.1 Ah).
     b) Sensor Protocol: CALCE logs lack an active thermocouple channel (filled at constant 23°C), 
        so the Arrhenius temperature gate could not activate its dynamic degradation adjustments.
     c) C-rate: CALCE discharges at 1C, whereas NASA discharges at varying low/moderate currents.
   - Academic Conclusion: High in-dataset cross-cell accuracy does not guarantee out-of-the-box 
     zero-shot transfer across different cell geometries, C-rates, and unmonitored thermal conditions.

     complex multi-sensor correlations in Hilbert space.
  3. Coupling local Conv1D temporal denoising with global Transformer self-attention.

This fusion of domain electrochemistry, quantum expressivity, and modern sequence modeling 
establishes TE-Q-Transformer as a state-of-the-art foundation for battery SOH estimation.

10.2 POSITIONAL ENCODING MODULE WITH MATHEMATICAL FORMULA
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION:
Discharge curves have distinct phases: (1) Initial Ohmic drop, (2) Voltage plateau, 
and (3) Severe concentration polarization collapse. Without temporal position tags, 
self-attention is permutation-equivariant and cannot distinguish early-cycle discharge 
from end-of-discharge.

We inject fixed sinusoidal positional encodings into sequence tokens:
  For position pos \in [0, L] and latent dimension index 2i, 2i+1 \in [0, d_model):
  
  PE(pos, 2i)   = \sin\left( \frac{pos}{10000^{2i / d_{\text{model}}}} \right)
  PE(pos, 2i+1) = \cos\left( \frac{pos}{10000^{2i / d_{\text{model}}}} \right)

  In logarithmic implementation form:
  \omega_i = \exp\left( -\frac{2i}{d_{\text{model}}} \ln(10000) \right)
  PE(pos, 2i)   = \sin(pos \cdot \omega_i)
  PE(pos, 2i+1) = \cos(pos \cdot \omega_i)

```python
class PositionalEncoding(nn.Module):
    """Sinusoidal Positional Encoding for preserving temporal cycle order."""
    def __init__(self, d_model: int, max_len: int = 1024) -> None:
        super().__init__()
        # Initialize zero tensor of shape [max_len, d_model]
        pe = torch.zeros(max_len, d_model)
        
        # Position index vector: [max_len, 1]
        position = torch.arange(0, max_len, dtype=torch.float32).unsqueeze(1)
        
        # Division term: \exp( (2i) * (-ln(10000) / d_model) )
        div_term = torch.exp(
            torch.arange(0, d_model, 2, dtype=torch.float32)
            * (-torch.log(torch.tensor(10000.0)) / d_model)
        )
        
        # Assign even indices (2i) to sine and odd indices (2i+1) to cosine
        pe[:, 0::2] = torch.sin(position * div_term)
        pe[:, 1::2] = torch.cos(position * div_term)
        
        # Register as non-trainable persistent buffer [1, max_len, d_model]
        self.register_buffer("pe", pe.unsqueeze(0))

    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        """Adds positional embeddings: Z_{pos} = X + PE[:, :seq_len]"""
        return x + self.pe[:, : x.size(1)]
```

10.3 QUANTUM EMBEDDING LAYER (ARRHENIUS GATE + 4-QUBIT VQC)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION:
1. Kelvin Conversion:
   T_K = \max(T_c + 273.15, 1.0)  \quad [K]

2. Arrhenius Chemical Kinetics:
   Constants: R = 8.314462618 J/(mol*K), T_ref = 298.15 K (25°C)
   Trainable Activation Energy parameters: Ea_sei, Ea_pl (scaled by 10,000 J/mol):
     E_{a,\text{sei}}^{\text{actual}} = E_{a,\text{sei}} \times 10^4
     E_{a,\text{pl}}^{\text{actual}}  = E_{a,\text{pl}} \times 10^4

   - High-temperature Solid Electrolyte Interphase (SEI) growth factor:
     \phi_{\text{sei}} = \exp\left( \frac{E_{a,\text{sei}}^{\text{actual}}}{R} \left( \frac{1}{T_{\text{ref}}} - \frac{1}{T_K} \right) \right)

   - Low-temperature metallic Lithium Plating factor:
     \phi_{\text{pl}} = \exp\left( \frac{E_{a,\text{pl}}^{\text{actual}}}{R} \left( \frac{1}{T_K} - \frac{1}{T_{\text{ref}}} \right) \right)

   - Temperature rotation angle for Qubit 3:
     \theta_T = \frac{\pi}{4} (\phi_{\text{sei}} + \phi_{\text{pl}})

   - Deterministic Angle encodings for Qubits 0, 1, 2:
     \theta_V = \pi \cdot V_{\text{norm}}, \quad \theta_I = \pi \cdot I_{\text{norm}}, \quad \theta_t = \pi \cdot t_{\text{norm}}

3. Quantum Unitary Operators in Hilbert Space \mathcal{H} = (\mathbb{C}^2)^{\otimes 4}:
   - State Encoding:
     U_{\text{enc}} = R_y(\theta_V) \otimes R_y(\theta_I) \otimes R_y(\theta_t) \otimes R_y(\theta_T)
     Where:
       R_y(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos(\theta/2) & -\sin(\theta/2) \\ \sin(\theta/2) & \cos(\theta/2) \end{pmatrix}

   - Rich Entangler Unitary U_{\text{rich}}:
     a) R_z phase rotation: R_z(\alpha) = \text{diag}(e^{-i\alpha/2}, e^{i\alpha/2})
     b) Nearest-neighbor CNOT entangling:
        \text{CNOT}_{q, q+1} = |0\rangle\langle 0| \otimes I + |1\rangle\langle 1| \otimes X
     c) Two-body Ising ZZ coupling:
        \text{IsingZZ}(\beta) = \exp\left(-i \frac{\beta}{2} (Z \otimes Z)\right) = \text{diag}(e^{-i\beta/2}, e^{i\beta/2}, e^{i\beta/2}, e^{-i\beta/2})
     d) Three-body non-linear Toffoli (CCNOT):
        \text{Toffoli}_{0,1,2} = (|11\rangle\langle 11| \otimes X) + ((I - |11\rangle\langle 11|) \otimes I)
     e) Hadamard superposition: H = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}

4. Pauli-Z Expectation Readout:
   \mathbf{q} = \left[ \langle\psi| Z_0 |\psi\rangle, \langle\psi| Z_1 |\psi\rangle, \langle\psi| Z_2 |\psi\rangle, \langle\psi| Z_3 |\psi\rangle \right]^T \in [-1, 1]^4

```python
class QuantumEmbeddingLayer(nn.Module):
    """Physics-Informed Quantum Embedding Layer with Arrhenius Temperature Gate."""
    def __init__(
        self,
        n_qubits: int = 4,
        q_device: str = "default.qubit",
        entangler_layers: int = 1,
        entangler_type: str = "basic",
        use_pauli_feature_map: bool = False,
        feature_map_reps: int = 1,
        feature_map_entangle: bool = True,
    ) -> None:
        super().__init__()
        self.n_qubits = n_qubits
        self.R = 8.314462618            # Universal gas constant [J / (mol * K)]
        self.T_ref = 298.15             # Reference temperature 25°C [Kelvin]
        self.entangler_type = entangler_type
        self.use_pauli_feature_map = use_pauli_feature_map
        self.feature_map_reps = feature_map_reps
        self.feature_map_entangle = feature_map_entangle

        # Trainable Arrhenius activation-energy parameters (initialized to 3.0)
        self.Ea_sei = nn.Parameter(torch.tensor(3.0, dtype=torch.float32))
        self.Ea_pl  = nn.Parameter(torch.tensor(3.0, dtype=torch.float32))

        # Trainable Quantum Entangler parameters
        self.entangler_weights = nn.Parameter(
            0.01 * torch.randn(entangler_layers, n_qubits, dtype=torch.float32)
        )
        self.entangler_rzz = nn.Parameter(
            0.01 * torch.randn(entangler_layers, max(1, n_qubits - 1), dtype=torch.float32)
        )
        
        # State-vector quantum simulator device
        dev = qml.device(q_device, wires=n_qubits)

        def _apply_pauli_feature_map(inputs: torch.Tensor) -> None:
            """Optional Pauli feature map with RX, RY, and CZ gates."""
            for _ in range(self.feature_map_reps):
                for i in range(n_qubits):
                    qml.RX(inputs[:, i], wires=i)
                    qml.RY(inputs[:, i], wires=i)
                if self.feature_map_entangle:
                    for i in range(n_qubits - 1):
                        qml.CZ(wires=[i, i + 1])

        def _apply_basic_entangler(entangler_weights: torch.Tensor) -> None:
            """Standard circular CNOT entanglement."""
            qml.BasicEntanglerLayers(entangler_weights, wires=range(n_qubits))

        def _apply_rich_entangler(
            entangler_weights: torch.Tensor, entangler_rzz: torch.Tensor
        ) -> None:
            """Rich Entangler: Rz + CNOT + IsingZZ + Toffoli + Hadamard + Rz."""
            for layer in range(entangler_weights.shape[0]):
                # 1. Rz rotation per wire
                for q in range(n_qubits):
                    qml.RZ(entangler_weights[layer, q], wires=q)
                # 2. Nearest-neighbor CNOT
                for q in range(n_qubits - 1):
                    qml.CNOT(wires=[q, q + 1])
                # 3. Parametric two-qubit IsingZZ coupling
                for q in range(n_qubits - 1):
                    qml.IsingZZ(entangler_rzz[layer, q], wires=[q, q + 1])
                # 4. Multi-body Toffoli 3-qubit gates
                if n_qubits >= 3:
                    qml.Toffoli(wires=[0, 1, 2])
                    if n_qubits >= 4:
                        qml.Toffoli(wires=[1, 2, 3])
                # 5. Hadamard superposition and final phase shift
                for q in range(n_qubits):
                    qml.Hadamard(wires=q)
                    qml.RZ(entangler_weights[layer, q], wires=q)

        @qml.qnode(dev, interface="torch", diff_method="backprop")
        def circuit(
            inputs: torch.Tensor,
            entangler_weights: torch.Tensor,
            entangler_rzz: torch.Tensor,
        ):
            """PennyLane QNode computing expectation values via backpropagation."""
            if self.use_pauli_feature_map:
                _apply_pauli_feature_map(inputs)
            
            # State preparation: Ry rotations on 4 wires
            qml.RY(inputs[:, 0], wires=0)
            qml.RY(inputs[:, 1], wires=1)
            qml.RY(inputs[:, 2], wires=2)
            qml.RY(inputs[:, 3], wires=3)

            # Entanglement mixing
            if self.entangler_type == "rich":
                _apply_rich_entangler(entangler_weights, entangler_rzz)
            else:
                _apply_basic_entangler(entangler_weights)

            # Measurement: Pauli-Z expectation values <Z_i>
            return tuple(qml.expval(qml.PauliZ(i)) for i in range(n_qubits))

        self.circuit = circuit

    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        """Executes forward pass of physics Arrhenius gate and quantum circuit.
        
        Input:  x of shape [B_flat, 4] where channels are [V, I, T_c, t_norm]
        Output: q_tensor of shape [B_flat, 4]
        """
        if x.ndim != 2 or x.shape[1] != 4:
            raise ValueError(f"Expected [B_flat, 4], got {tuple(x.shape)}")

        out_device = x.device
        out_dtype = x.dtype

        # 1. Deterministic angle mappings for V, I, and Time: theta = value * pi
        voltage_angle = x[:, 0] * torch.pi
        current_angle = x[:, 1] * torch.pi
        time_angle    = x[:, 3] * torch.pi

        # 2. Physics-Informed Arrhenius Temperature Formulation:
        temp_c = x[:, 2]                                           # Raw Celsius (°C)
        temp_k = torch.clamp(temp_c + 273.15, min=1.0)             # Absolute Kelvin (K)
        inv_t = 1.0 / temp_k                                       # 1 / T_K
        inv_t_ref = 1.0 / self.T_ref                               # 1 / 298.15
        
        Ea_sei_actual = self.Ea_sei * 10000.0                      # Scale to J/mol
        Ea_pl_actual  = self.Ea_pl  * 10000.0                      # Scale to J/mol

        # SEI growth rate factor (increases when temp_k > T_ref)
        sei_term = torch.exp((Ea_sei_actual / self.R) * (inv_t_ref - inv_t))
        
        # Lithium plating rate factor (increases when temp_k < T_ref)
        plating_term = torch.exp((Ea_pl_actual / self.R) * (inv_t - inv_t_ref))
        
        # Combined thermal degradation scalar phi
        phi = sei_term + plating_term
        
        # Map physical degradation phi to quantum rotation angle theta_T
        theta_temp = torch.pi * phi / 4.0

        # Stack into 4D angle vector
        angles = torch.stack([voltage_angle, current_angle, time_angle, theta_temp], dim=1)

        # Quantum circuit execution (PennyLane runs on CPU state-vector simulator)
        angles_cpu = angles.to("cpu")
        entangler_cpu = self.entangler_weights.to("cpu")
        entangler_rzz_cpu = self.entangler_rzz.to("cpu")
        
        q_out = self.circuit(angles_cpu, entangler_cpu, entangler_rzz_cpu)
        
        # Stack 4 expectation values and map back to device
        q_tensor = torch.stack(q_out, dim=1).to(device=out_device, dtype=out_dtype)
        return q_tensor
```

10.4 MAIN TE-Q-TRANSFORMER MODEL PIPELINE WITH FULL FORWARD PASS MATH
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MATHEMATICAL STEPS OF FORWARD PASS:
1. Input Flattening:
   X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512 \times 4} \xrightarrow{\text{reshape}} X_{\text{flat}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(512B) \times 4}

2. Quantum Embedding:
   \mathbf{q}_{\text{flat}} = \text{QuantumEmbedding}(X_{\text{flat}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{(512B) \times 4}

3. Latent Space Projection:
   \mathbf{h}_{\text{flat}} = \mathbf{q}_{\text{flat}} W_p^T + \mathbf{b}_p \in \mathbb{R}^{(512B) \times 64}
   \mathbf{H}_{\text{seq}} = \text{reshape}(\mathbf{h}_{\text{flat}}, [B, 512, 64])

4. Temporal Local Smoothing (Conv1D):
   Transpose to channel-first: \mathbf{H}^T \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 64 \times 512}
   1D convolution with kernel size k=3:
     \mathbf{H}_{\text{smooth}}(b, c, t) = \sum_{\tau=-1}^{1} W_{\text{conv}}(c, c, \tau) \cdot \mathbf{H}^T(b, c, t + \tau)
   Transpose back: \mathbf{H}_{\text{refined}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 512 \times 64}

5. Learnable [CLS] Token Prepending:
   Z_0 = \left[ \mathbf{z}_{\text{cls}} \;\|\; \mathbf{H}_{\text{refined}}(:, 1, :) \;\|\; \dots \;\|\; \mathbf{H}_{\text{refined}}(:, 512, :) \right] \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 513 \times 64}

6. Positional Encoding Addition:
   Z_{\text{pos}} = Z_0 + \text{PE}_{0:513} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 513 \times 64}

7. Stacked Multi-Head Transformer Encoding (3 Layers, Pre-LN):
   For layer l = 1, 2, 3:
     Z^{(l)}_{\text{norm1}} = \text{LayerNorm}(Z^{(l-1)})
     Z^{(l)}_{\text{attn}}  = Z^{(l-1)} + \text{MHA}(Q = Z^{(l)}_{\text{norm1}}, K = Z^{(l)}_{\text{norm1}}, V = Z^{(l)}_{\text{norm1}})
     Z^{(l)}_{\text{norm2}} = \text{LayerNorm}(Z^{(l)}_{\text{attn}})
     Z^{(l)}                = Z^{(l)}_{\text{attn}} + \text{FFN}(Z^{(l)}_{\text{norm2}})
   Output: Z^{(3)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 513 \times 64}

8. CLS Pooling:
   \mathbf{z}_{\text{cls}}^{\text{out}} = Z^{(3)}[:, 0, :] \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 64}

9. SOH Output MLP Regression:
   \hat{y} = W_2 \cdot \text{GELU}(W_1 \mathbf{z}_{\text{cls}}^{\text{out}} + \mathbf{b}_1) + b_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1}
   \text{SOH} = \text{squeeze}(\hat{y}, -1) \in \mathbb{R}^B

```python
class TEQTransformer(nn.Module):
    """Full TE-Q-Transformer model for battery State of Health prediction."""
    def __init__(self, cfg: TEQTransformerConfig | None = None) -> None:
        super().__init__()
        self.cfg = cfg or TEQTransformerConfig()
        
        # Runtime architectural assertions
        if self.cfg.input_dim != 4:
            raise ValueError("This model expects exactly 4 input features.")
        if self.cfg.quantum_dim != 4:
            raise ValueError("quantum_dim must be 4 to match the 4 input channels.")
        if self.cfg.d_model % self.cfg.n_heads != 0:
            raise ValueError("d_model must be divisible by n_heads.")
        if self.cfg.d_model <= self.cfg.quantum_dim:
            raise ValueError("d_model should be larger than quantum_dim after projection.")
        if self.cfg.pooling not in {"cls", "mean"}:
            raise ValueError("pooling must be either 'cls' or 'mean'.")
        if self.cfg.use_cls_token and self.cfg.pooling != "cls":
            raise ValueError("use_cls_token=True requires pooling='cls'.")
        if not self.cfg.use_cls_token and self.cfg.pooling != "mean":
            raise ValueError("use_cls_token=False requires pooling='mean'.")
        if self.cfg.use_temporal_smooth and self.cfg.temporal_kernel_size % 2 == 0:
            raise ValueError("temporal_kernel_size should be odd to preserve sequence length.")
        if self.cfg.use_gru_smoother and self.cfg.use_temporal_smooth:
            raise ValueError("Enable only one temporal module: GRU smoother or Conv1D smoother.")
        if self.cfg.entangler_type not in {"basic", "rich"}:
            raise ValueError("entangler_type must be 'basic' or 'rich'.")
        if self.cfg.head_hidden_dim <= 0:
            raise ValueError("head_hidden_dim must be positive.")

        # 1. Quantum Embedding Layer
        self.quantum_embed = QuantumEmbeddingLayer(
            n_qubits=self.cfg.quantum_dim,
            q_device=self.cfg.q_device,
            entangler_layers=self.cfg.entangler_layers,
            entangler_type=self.cfg.entangler_type,
            use_pauli_feature_map=self.cfg.use_pauli_feature_map,
            feature_map_reps=self.cfg.feature_map_reps,
            feature_map_entangle=self.cfg.feature_map_entangle,
        )
        
        # 2. Quantum Projection Linear Layer: 4 -> 64
        self.quantum_proj = nn.Linear(self.cfg.quantum_dim, self.cfg.d_model)

        # 3. Learnable [CLS] Token Parameter
        self.cls_token = (
            nn.Parameter(torch.randn(1, 1, self.cfg.d_model))
            if self.cfg.use_cls_token
            else None
        )
        
        # 4. Positional Encoding Buffer
        self.pos_encoder = (
            PositionalEncoding(self.cfg.d_model, max_len=self.cfg.seq_len + 2)
            if self.cfg.use_positional_encoding
            else None
        )

        # 5. Local Temporal Smoother (Conv1D with kernel size k=3)
        if self.cfg.use_temporal_smooth:
            self.temporal_smooth = nn.Conv1d(
                in_channels=self.cfg.d_model,
                out_channels=self.cfg.d_model,
                kernel_size=self.cfg.temporal_kernel_size,
                padding=self.cfg.temporal_kernel_size // 2,
                bias=False,
            )
        else:
            self.temporal_smooth = nn.Identity()

        # Optional GRU Smoother (disabled in default configuration)
        if self.cfg.use_gru_smoother:
            self.gru_smoother = nn.GRU(
                input_size=self.cfg.d_model,
                hidden_size=self.cfg.d_model,
                num_layers=self.cfg.gru_num_layers,
                dropout=self.cfg.gru_dropout if self.cfg.gru_num_layers > 1 else 0.0,
                batch_first=True,
            )
        else:
            self.gru_smoother = None

        # Optional Residual MLP (disabled in default configuration)
        self.residual_mlp = (
            nn.Sequential(
                nn.Linear(self.cfg.d_model, self.cfg.residual_mlp_dim),
                nn.GELU(),
                nn.Linear(self.cfg.residual_mlp_dim, self.cfg.d_model),
            )
            if self.cfg.use_residual_mlp
            else None
        )

        # 6. Global Multi-Head Transformer Encoder Stack (3 layers, Pre-LN)
        encoder_layer = nn.TransformerEncoderLayer(
            d_model=self.cfg.d_model,
            nhead=self.cfg.n_heads,
            dim_feedforward=self.cfg.dim_feedforward,
            dropout=self.cfg.dropout,
            activation="gelu",
            batch_first=True,
            norm_first=True,
        )
        self.transformer = nn.TransformerEncoder(encoder_layer, num_layers=self.cfg.n_layers)
        
        # 7. SOH MLP Regression Head
        self.head = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(self.cfg.d_model, self.cfg.head_hidden_dim),
            nn.GELU(),
            nn.Dropout(self.cfg.dropout),
            nn.Linear(self.cfg.head_hidden_dim, 1),
        )

    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        """Full end-to-end forward pass of TE-Q-Transformer.
        
        Args:
            x: Input sensor tensor [Batch, Sequence_Len, Channels] = [B, 512, 4]
        Returns:
            soh: Estimated battery State of Health scalar [B]
        """
        if x.ndim != 3 or x.shape[-1] != 4:
            raise ValueError(f"Expected [B, L, 4], got {tuple(x.shape)}")
        batch_size, seq_len, _ = x.shape
        if seq_len != self.cfg.seq_len:
            raise ValueError(f"Expected sequence length {self.cfg.seq_len}, got {seq_len}.")

        # Step 1: Flatten sequence for parallel time-step quantum processing: [B*512, 4]
        x_flat = x.reshape(batch_size * seq_len, 4)
        
        # Step 2: Compute Arrhenius physics and 4-qubit Pauli-Z expectations: [B*512, 4]
        q_features = self.quantum_embed(x_flat)
        
        # Step 3: Project quantum expectations into latent d_model space: [B*512, 64]
        q_features = self.quantum_proj(q_features)
        
        # Step 4: Reorganize back into sequence format: [B, 512, 64]
        q_sequence = q_features.reshape(batch_size, seq_len, self.cfg.d_model)

        # Step 5: Optional GRU smoothing (if enabled)
        if self.gru_smoother is not None:
            q_sequence, _ = self.gru_smoother(q_sequence)

        # Step 6: 1D Temporal Convolutional filter (local noise smoothing)
        q_sequence = q_sequence.transpose(1, 2)            # Shape: [B, 64, 512]
        q_sequence = self.temporal_smooth(q_sequence)      # Shape: [B, 64, 512]
        q_sequence = q_sequence.transpose(1, 2)            # Shape: [B, 512, 64]

        # Step 7: Optional Residual MLP connection (if enabled)
        if self.residual_mlp is not None:
            q_sequence = q_sequence + self.residual_mlp(q_sequence)

        # Step 8: Prepend learnable [CLS] token: [B, 513, 64]
        if self.cls_token is not None:
            cls_tokens = self.cls_token.expand(batch_size, -1, -1)
            q_sequence = torch.cat([cls_tokens, q_sequence], dim=1)

        # Step 9: Inject sinusoidal positional encodings: [B, 513, 64]
        if self.pos_encoder is not None:
            q_sequence = self.pos_encoder(q_sequence)

        # Step 10: Stacked Transformer self-attention: [B, 513, 64]
        transformed = self.transformer(q_sequence)
        
        # Step 11: Pooling - Extract [CLS] token at index 0: [B, 64]
        if self.cfg.pooling == "cls":
            pooled = transformed[:, 0]
        else:
            pooled = transformed.mean(dim=1)
            
        # Step 12: Regression Head: Linear -> GELU -> Linear -> [B, 1]
        soh = self.head(pooled)
        
        # Squeeze last dimension to return scalar SOH per batch item: [B]
        return soh.squeeze(-1)


def rich_entangler_config() -> TEQTransformerConfig:
    """Convenience helper returning the validated reference configuration."""
    return TEQTransformerConfig(entangler_type="rich")
```
